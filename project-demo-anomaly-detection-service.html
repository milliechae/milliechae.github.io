PAIN POINT

After an anomaly is detected, the real challenge begins.

Fabs generate thousands of alerts a day. Operators still spent most of their time working out which machine, which chamber and why, before they could act.

✦ DESIGN CHALLENGE

How might we help operators understand an anomaly as quickly as they detect it?

Turn a wall of raw alerts into a clear path to the root cause.

RESEARCH

Understanding how operators investigate anomalies.

I interviewed operators and mapped their workflow on the fab floor, focusing on how they decided what to do when time mattered.

01

Finding the affected equipment takes too long.

Pinpointing the chamber meant several screens and decoding long equipment IDs.

02

AI detects anomalies, but doesn't provide enough context.

An alert said something was wrong, not what changed or whether to act now.

03

Critical information is scattered across multiple views.

One anomaly meant switching between dashboards, logs and monitoring screens.

04

Repetitive equipment management slows daily operations.

The same settings were repeated machine by machine, inviting mistakes.

Design Solution

Based on the research, I redesigned the monitoring experience so operators move from detection to understanding to action without breaking flow.

Make anomaly status instantly recognizable.

Consistent colours replace tables and numbers, so abnormal states read at a glance.

Keep investigation in a single workflow.

Traces, patterns and equipment details sit on one screen, so investigating never means leaving it.

Reduce repetitive operational tasks.

Bulk configuration applies routine setup to many machines at once.

Impact

Faster decisions, with more confidence.

25%↓
Incident response time

Detection, investigation and response now happen in one flow, with no IDs to decode and no systems to jump between.

Reflection

Trust matters more than predictions.

An AI alert is only useful if people trust it. Showing what changed and where is what turned alerts into decisions. Next time I'd test with operators earlier to learn how much context they need before they act.
